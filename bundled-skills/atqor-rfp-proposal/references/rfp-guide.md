# Buyer-issued RFP adaptation

This branch is **new procurement guidance**, not a claim that the supplied Arvind proposal contains an RFP. Use the source's clarity, section hierarchy and table design, while expressing the buyer's requirements and evaluation process.

## Mode and branding

Identify the issuing organization, project, procurement contact, reference/version, audience and procurement stage. Use the issuer's actual logo and confidentiality label. Preserve atQor branding only when atQor is the issuer or the user explicitly wants atQor-authored advisory collateral. Never transfer atQor Microsoft awards/badges to a buyer. If no issuer logo is supplied, continue the content draft and flag that asset; do not invent it.

## Adapted outline

1. Cover, document control and contents.
2. Issuer/background, business need and project objectives.
3. Existing environment, data/platform constraints, assumptions and buyer-provided inputs.
4. Scope and requirements, grouped by business/functional, technical/integration/data, security/privacy, operational/service and delivery requirements as needed.
5. Expected deliverables, milestones, dependencies, handover/training and acceptance approach.
6. Bidder eligibility and relevant experience/evidence requirements.
7. Response structure, mandatory forms, technical/commercial separation if required, compliance/deviation matrix and pricing format.
8. Evaluation process, mandatory gates and weighted criteria if supplied or clearly proposed. Do not invent approved weights or eligibility restrictions. Proposed weights must sum to 100%.
9. Procurement schedule: issue, clarification deadline, answers/addenda, submission deadline/time zone, demonstrations, award/contract target if known.
10. Commercial/contract requirements: pricing inclusions, tax/currency, quote validity, licenses/cloud consumption, acceptance/payment basis, support and change-control expectations as appropriate.
11. Submission instructions, contact/channel, confidentiality and bidder declarations/acknowledgment.

Do not include a bilateral executed SoW sign-off as if an RFP selects the supplier. A bidder declaration or later contracting requirement fits this stage.

## Requirement writing

Use a stable ID, explicit need, priority, measurable acceptance/evidence and dependencies. Example method: “[REQ-ID] The proposed solution shall [capability] for [users/data boundary], measured by [test/evidence].” Use `shall` for required outcomes and distinguish optional features. Avoid specifying a supplier's architecture unnecessarily; state mandatory platform constraints only when the buyer actually requires them.

Ask bidders to disclose assumptions, exclusions, gaps, integrations, access boundaries, delivery model and support. Pricing schedules should separate implementation, recurring cloud/license costs, support, options, taxes and expenses on a comparable basis. Where workload is unknown, request unit rates and a stated sizing model rather than pretending a fixed consumption figure is agreed.

## Validation

Every mandatory requirement should have a requested response/evidence field and evaluation or acceptance route. Confirm that submission dates/time zones, prerequisites and delivery windows agree. Do not copy supplier names, historical amounts, awards, staff, signatures, payment deadlines or restrictive exclusions from the reference into buyer requirements without instruction. Where procurement facts are missing, present them as proposed or unconfirmed; do not invent official deadlines, contacts or legal approval.
